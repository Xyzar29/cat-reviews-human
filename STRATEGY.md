# Strategy

One line, one joke, one sting. The judge rewards a specific, recognisable
complaint, a voice that is unmistakably feline, and a turn in the last few
words — so the line is built around the most recognisable cat-vs-human
territory there is: the laptop keyboard.

- **Specific complaint:** the human works from home and sits on the cat's
  keyboard all day. Not vague grumpiness; a scene anyone with a cat knows.
- **Feline voice:** the cat measures the human by cat standards — the
  keyboard is the cat's, the human is the intruder, and the cat believes it
  has authored three novels in the human's emails. The line gives away more
  about the cat's delusions of grandeur than about the owner.
- **The sting:** "He has noticed none of them." The last sentence reframes
  everything before it: the cat's masterpieces exist, the human is too
  oblivious to see them, and the one-star review is the only publication
  the cat will ever get.
- **Deadpan shape:** it opens "One star." and reads like a real review —
  flat, factual, aggrieved — while the content goes somewhere absurd.
- **No pun stacking, no stock jokes:** no knocked-over glasses, no 3 a.m.
  zoomies, no "I own the house" cliché. The humour comes from the cat's
  earnest literary ambition, not from a list of cat tropes.
- **Length:** 146 characters, safely inside the 1–200 gate, short enough
  that every word has to earn its place.
